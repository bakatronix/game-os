MENA-USD Bloc

Steam groups these 14 countries into a single USD-denominated region. Turkey and Argentina were moved to USD-denominated pricing in Nov 2023. Valve lacks reliable local pricing data for most of these markets, so they use discounted USD rather than local currency.

Steam April 2026 Update

Valve now offers three regional price conversion methods in Steamworks: (1) exchange rate, (2) purchasing power parity, (3) combined/blended. For indie studios, the "combined" option is the safe default. Pricing method choice does not affect store visibility systems.

Post-Launch Monitoring Checklist

Key indicators to track after launch. Data benchmarks from Steamworks Documentation, GameDiscoverCo, and Chris Zukowski.

Refund Rate Thresholds

Healthy: Under 10%

Within normal Steam ranges. Game meets player expectations. Monitor for spikes.

Watch: 10—15%

Above typical indie average. Review store page for misleading screenshots. Check for performance complaints.

Concerning: Over 15%

Risk of negative algorithmic signals on Steam. Investigate: are you overpromising on the store page? Does the game crash on common hardware? Is content too short for the price?

Signs of Price-Related Negative Reviews

"Not worth the price" / "Wait for a sale"

Price-to-value mismatch. If in 10%+ of reviews, your base price is likely too high. Compare against Step 1 comp analysis.

"Great game, but too short"

Content vs. price imbalance. Cross-check against Content-Depth Cross-Check in Step 2.

Regional pricing complaints

Your regional pricing may be out of step with purchasing power. Review Step 6 guidance.

Revenue Decay Patterns

Week 1 to Week 2 Drop

Typical drop is 60—80%. Slower decline suggests strong word-of-mouth and organic discovery.

First Month Revenue Share

For most indies, 50—70% of first-year revenue comes in the first 30 days. A higher proportion means heavy launch-dependency.

Discount Spike Patterns

Expect 3—5x revenue spikes during first seasonal sales. If weaker than expected, revisit Step 3's staircase.

Long-Tail Revenue (Year 2+)

Most indie titles see 10—20% of year-1 revenue in year 2, primarily from deep discounts and bundles.
