How the scores are made

  • Satisfaction is the share of Steam reviews that are positive, across all languages. The review history chart uses Steam’s monthly review counts. When peers load, it also shows where that share ranks among similar games.
  • Engagement averages two parts: median lifetime playtime from SteamSpy (10 hours scores full marks), and the median share of players holding each achievement (30% scores full marks). These are rules of thumb and vary by genre: a short narrative game will score lower on playtime without being worse.
  • Reach ranks the game’s review count against peers: every game on Steam with the same two most distinctive tags, released within nine months either side of it. If peers can’t be loaded it uses a log scale instead (100,000 reviews scores full marks).
  • Owner ranges and playtime come from SteamSpy, an independent estimate. Copies are estimated at about 30 per review.
  • Steam no longer lets tools read individual reviews, so there is no keyword cloud. Read the reviews themselves on Steam for the “why”.
  • The Sean Ellis test asks your players “How would you feel if you could no longer play this game?”. If 40% or more answer “very disappointed”, that is a common sign of product-market fit.
