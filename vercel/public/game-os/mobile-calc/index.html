Store fees: check the current terms

Apple’s Small Business Program lists a 15% commission for developers with up to US$1M in proceeds the previous year. Google Play lists 15% on the first US$1M a year and 30% above it, and 15% on auto-renewing subscriptions, with different rates in some regions. Terms change: read Apple’s page and Google’s page on the day you build your plan.

How this calculator works

What it does

It builds a retention curve from your day 1 and day 30 retention, then adds up what each install is worth over the horizon you choose: in-app purchases, ads, subscriptions and any upfront price, after store fees, tax and refunds, minus variable cost. It compares that value with what the install cost you.

Lifetime value (LTV) here means profit per install before acquisition cost. Profit per install is LTV minus the blended cost per install. Payback is the first day on which cumulative LTV covers the blended cost.

The funding plan then spends your monthly acquisition budget, buys installs at each case’s cost per install (plus organic installs on top), and lets each month’s installs earn for 12 months. Revenue is added to cash, running costs and the round are applied, and the plan shows when cash runs out.

Assumptions that can mislead you

  • Retention beyond day 30 is the curve’s extension, not data. Real games often retain differently later on.
  • Paid and organic installs are assumed to behave the same. Organic installs cost nothing and lower your blended cost.
  • Ad and purchase revenue per active user is held flat. In practice it changes with player age and level.
  • Subscription revenue is charged at day 0 and every 30 days after, with the renewal rate applied each month.
  • One store fee is used for all sales. Fees differ by store, region and yearly revenue: use an honest blend.
  • No regional pricing, seasonality, featured placements, or paying versus non-paying players.
  • Funding plan: cash basis, monthly. Earnings after an install’s 12th month are left out to stay conservative. No payout delay (stores usually pay one to two months later), no tax on profit, no hiring changes, no second round. The model stops after 36 months.
  • The acquisition budget is spent whether or not it pays back. A real team would cut spend if payback were poor; the stress case shows what happens if it does not.

Glossary

Install: one download. DAU: daily active users. ARPDAU: average revenue per daily active user. CPI: cost per install. eCPM: ad revenue per 1,000 impressions. LTV: lifetime value, here per install and before acquisition cost. Blended cost: what an average install costs once organic installs count as zero.
