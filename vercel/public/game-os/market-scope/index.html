Does an audience exist for your game, and does it pay? Pick the Steam tags that describe your game and this sizes the space from every game released under them in the last three years.

Your space

Two or three tags work best: your genre plus what makes it different. Steam counts a game only if it has all the tags you pick.

    Estimate assumptions

    How the numbers are made

    • Source: Steam’s store search, newest releases first, US store. Only full games (no DLC, soundtracks or bundles).
    • Revenue estimate: reviews × copies per review × US list price × average price paid. It is gross: before Steam’s cut (30%, falling to 25% and 20% above US$10M and US$50M), refunds, VAT and any publisher share.
    • Games with fewer than 10 reviews show no review count on Steam; they are counted as 5 reviews. Free-to-play games are counted as releases but have no revenue estimate.
    • Revenue spread uses paid games released 6 to 36 months ago, so new releases that haven’t had time to sell don’t drag it down.
    • Crowding counts every release. The last two quarters are still maturing: their revenue will grow.
    • Tag-pair gaps use the top tags Steam shows for each game in your results (about seven per game), so smaller tags are undercounted. Groups of fewer than five games are left out; treat small groups as leads to check, not proof.
    • Reviews per copy differ by genre, price and audience. Use these figures to compare spaces with each other, not as a forecast for your game.
